## Reading the numbers

1. **Lateral compression is a property of the viewing geometry (RP-5 confirmed in the model).** From an elevated side-on camera, lateral metres project at roughly height/depth of longitudinal metres: ≈ 0.43 in front of the stands at 18 m height, ≈ 0.05 on the back straight 340 m away (3° elevation). With `SW = SL·sw_ratio` the lane gap in front of the stands is ≈ 0.4 SW, below `same_tier_sw = 0.6`: adjacent lanes merge **by construction**, not through mis-calibration. Wherever a section is seen at a few degrees of elevation, no lateral scale can separate lanes; only another camera position can. OD-1x step 4 must record, per track section, which camera covers it and the apparent fence–rail separation.
2. **The SL definition is view-dependent.** Where the camera looks along the track (view angle ≈ 10°) the bbox long side is the vehicle height, not its length (≈ 43 px instead of 80): longitudinal units collapse there. The main wide pan is side-on enough most of the time, but the pipeline should treat low view angles as degenerate.
3. **A linear two-line scale (RP-19 as first stated) fails under perspective.** The fence is at ~15 m depth and the rail at ~40 m, so one pixel factor for 25 m of width is wrong at the pack. A **1-D projective lateral coordinate** (rail, fence, and their vanishing point on a horizontal horizon) is exact in the model and is not a homography to track coordinates: it needs no intrinsics and no template, only the two lines and the width in SL (`track_width_sl`).
4. **The motion-based axis (§3.1) is biased by camera work, not just by jitter (RP-21, RP-22).** With a well-tracking camera the runners' residual image motion is pan wobble plus zoom flow and carries ~5–6° of systematic axis error even with zero jitter. The projected **rail-line direction** gives the axis exactly and must not be smoothed (it rotates as the camera pans).
5. **Bends need a local datum, not a local axis (RP-20).** One direction for a 5 SL pack mid-bend leaves 0.4–0.8 SW of lateral error at 110–200 m radius; measuring each runner against the nearest rail/fence segment (polyline datum) reduces it to ≈ 0.1 SW and restores full tier resolution. `axis_neighbourhood_k` is unnecessary once the rail datum exists.
6. **The spec's centroid (mid-x, max-y) carries a pan-dependent lateral bias of up to ~1 SW (RP-23).** The bbox bottom is the vehicle's near ground edge (half a width off the centre line) and that edge slopes under pan, so the mid-x point sits further below it. Relative lanes survive; the absolute attachment to the rail fails at its closed 1.0 SW tolerance. A foot-point correction (slope removal + half-width shift with the sign of the lateral direction) brings the residual to ≈ 0.1 SW at 26° pan and ≈ 0.45 SW at 46°.
7. **Jitter sensitivity (RP-21) is unchanged in kind:** with the rail-line axis the axis error is zero and the remaining tier losses at 1–2 px jitter come from centroid noise against a ~0.4 SW lane gap. Lateral resolution is budget-limited by the viewing geometry before it is limited by the detector.

_Assumptions to replace with measurements: bend radius, track width, camera height and setback per section, vehicle length and width, lane spacing. OD-1x step 4 measures lane gap, fence–rail separation and view angle per section; a track plan gives width and radii._
